Self-hosted render farm for Remotion

Your team already owns a render farm.It's just asleep.

A Remotion render pins one machine for an hour while four other laptops sit at three percent. teadiOS puts the queue in Postgres, a container on every laptop, and the finished file on your CDN.

Same jobs, same total machine time, same electricity. The only thing that changed is how many laptops were awake to take a turn.

Three stages, and then you stop thinking about it.

stage · deploy

Put the console up

A static site on Vercel pointed at a Postgres database. There is no server and no database password in it — the browser talks straight to Postgres, and row-level security decides what it may see.

stage · enlist

Lend a laptop

One container per machine. It registers itself, sends a heartbeat, and only takes work when the laptop is plugged in and has disk to spare. Close the lid whenever you like.

stage · submit

Hand it a brief

Anyone on the team submits from the browser. The next free laptop takes it, streams its log back live, and pushes the finished file to your CDN before it calls the job done.

What happens when someone closes their laptop mid-render.

lid closes at frame 4,000
The heartbeat lapses, another machine picks the job up from the stage it reached rather than from zero, and the first laptop is fenced out of writing when it eventually wakes up. Both would otherwise bill.
two laptops, one job
Cannot happen. Work is claimed with SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, so Postgres itself is the load balancer. There is no broker to run and nothing else to page you at 3am.
render finished, upload failed
That is a failed job, not a finished one. The database refuses to mark a run complete without a file on your CDN — a video that exists only on the laptop that made it is the thing this exists to prevent.
everyone went home
The console says so, in words. A queued job with no fleet online looks exactly like healthy progress otherwise, and will sit there all weekend.
someone hits cancel
The browser can only mark the row. It cannot signal a process on a colleague's machine, so the worker watches its own row and stops itself.

Where it won't help.

  • You work alone. One laptop is one lane. There is nothing to spread across, and you will just have added a database.
  • Your renders take seconds. Workers poll every two seconds. If the job is shorter than the poll, the poll is the job.
  • You need a deadline you can promise. These are people's laptops. They close them, they board flights, they run out of disk.
  • Remotion is not MIT-licensed. It is free for individuals and small companies and paid above that. Check where your team lands before you point it at one.
